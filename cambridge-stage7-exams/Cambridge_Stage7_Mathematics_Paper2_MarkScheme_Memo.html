Cambridge Assessment Marking Symbols Guide (Paper 2):
M Marks: Method marks for showing correct algebraic/numerical procedure, calculator setup, or formula usage.
A Marks: Accuracy marks awarded only if the corresponding M mark has been earned.
B Marks: Independent marks awarded for correct values, units, or statements without working needed.
ft: Follow-through marks awarded for logically correct work following an earlier numerical slip.

💡 Teacher & Tutor Pedagogical Coaching Guide for Paper 2

Paper 2 permits calculators, but Cambridge tests conceptual problem-solving, not just computation. Here is how to guide Justin:

1. Multi-Step Equations (Q10b) — The Double Negative Warning

In \(3(2y + 5) - 4(y - 1) = 31\), learners with ADHD often write \(-4y - 4\). Highlight the minus sign in red: \(-4 \times -1 = \mathbf{+4}\). Teach Justin to whisper: "A negative times a negative makes a happy positive!"

2. Compound L-Shapes (Q5a) — Slice It With a Dotted Pencil Line

Before doing any arithmetic, prompt Justin: "Don't calculate yet! Draw a dashed pencil line to cut the L into two simple rectangles." Once it is two distinct rectangles (colored with highlighters if helpful), the area is straightforward addition.

3. Scale Drawings (Q7) — The 2-Step Unit Ladder

Going from map cm to real km involves two steps: first multiply by the scale factor to get centimetres (\(8.4 \times 25\,000 = 210\,000\text{ cm}\)), then divide by \(100\) to reach metres (\(2100\text{ m}\)), and divide by \(1000\) to reach kilometres (\(2.1\text{ km}\)). Show Justin the unit ladder!

4. Frequency Table Averages (Q8) — Don't Divide by 5!

The most common Cambridge mistake is dividing the sum by 5 (the number of score categories) instead of 20 (the total frequency of students). Have Justin verify: "How many students are in the class? 20! So we must divide by 20."
